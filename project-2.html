Preliminary Mission Analysis for a Transfer from Earth to Near-Earth Object 65803 Didymos

MAE 146: Astronautics — Project, Problem 4 · Skyler Cao, University of California, Irvine · December 6, 2025

Download Full Report

Abstract

This report presents a preliminary mission analysis for a spacecraft transfer from Earth to the near-Earth asteroid 65803 Didymos under restricted two-body dynamics. Heliocentric states of Earth and Didymos are propagated from Keplerian elements using an in-house Kepler propagator, and impulsive transfers are computed by solving Lambert's problem with universal variables under a zero-sphere-of-influence assumption. A reference transfer departing at t_ref + 12 days and arriving at t_ref + 412 days requires a total ΔV of 20.0300 km/s. A porkchop plot over a 1000-day window with 5-day resolution shows that substantially cheaper prograde transfers exist; the lowest-cost solution found departs at t_ref + 600 days and arrives at t_ref + 1000 days with a total ΔV of 7.8600 km/s, a reduction of roughly 61% relative to the reference case.

1. Introduction

Near-Earth objects (NEOs) are attractive targets for science, planetary defense, and resource-utilization missions. Didymos, the primary of the binary system targeted by the DART and Hera missions, has a moderately eccentric (e = 0.384) and inclined (i = 3.41°) orbit that crosses the region between Earth and Mars. Because its orbit differs substantially from Earth's, the cost of a rendezvous varies strongly with departure and arrival epochs, making a trade study over the launch window essential.

The objectives of this analysis are to:

  1. Determine the heliocentric position and velocity of Earth and Didymos at specified epochs
  2. Compute and visualize an Earth–Didymos transfer for a prescribed departure and arrival date
  3. Generate a porkchop plot of total ΔV over a 1000-day window
  4. Identify and plot a minimum-ΔV transfer from the porkchop plot

2.2 Orbit Propagation

States are propagated analytically along Keplerian arcs. The initial true anomaly is converted to eccentric anomaly E via tan(E/2) = √[(1−e)/(1+e)]·tan(θ/2), then to mean anomaly via Kepler's equation M = E − e·sin E. The mean anomaly is advanced by M(t) = M₀ + √(μ/a³)·Δt, and the inverse problem M → E → θ is solved with a Newton–Raphson iteration (tolerance 10⁻¹²). The resulting elements are converted to inertial Cartesian state with the perifocal-to-inertial rotation R₃(−Ω)R₁(−i)R₃(−ω).

2.3 Lambert's Problem

Transfers are computed with the universal-variable formulation of Lambert's problem. With Δν the transfer angle (prograde), A = sin Δν · √[r₁r₂/(1−cos Δν)], and y(z) = r₁ + r₂ + A[zS(z)−1]/√C(z). The time of flight is matched by finding the root z of F(z) = (1/√μ)[(y/C)^(3/2)·S + A√y] − Δt = 0, where C(z) and S(z) are the Stumpff functions. A Newton iteration safeguarded by bisection is used with tolerance ε = 10⁻⁸. The Lagrange coefficients f = 1 − y/r₁, g = A√(y/μ), ġ = 1 − y/r₂ give the terminal velocities v₁ = (r₂ − f·r₁)/g and v₂ = (ġ·r₂ − r₁)/g.

2.4 Delta-V Model

Assuming a zero sphere of influence at both bodies, the total impulsive cost is ΔV = ‖v₁ − v_E(t₀)‖₂ + ‖v₂ − v_D(t_f)‖₂.

2.5 Porkchop Grid

Departure and arrival days t₀, t_f ∈ [0, 1000] were sampled at 5-day intervals (201×201 grid). Body states were precomputed once per grid time, and Lambert's problem was solved for every pair with t_f − t₀ > 100 days (prograde only). Cases where the solver failed or returned a non-finite ΔV were left blank.

The arrival burn dominates. Didymos is at r ≈ 2.0 AU when the spacecraft arrives and moves in a very different direction than the transfer arc, which makes this timing poor.

3.4 Porkchop Plot

The total ΔV (clipped at 25.0000 km/s) was computed as a function of departure and arrival day. The cost is strongly periodic in departure date, reflecting the synodic relationship between Earth and Didymos, and the reference transfer lies in a high-cost region. The grid-wide minimum occurs at t₀ = 600 d and t_f = 1000 d with ΔV = 7.86 km/s. An "island" structure of cheap transfers is visible near the lower-right of the feasible region.

Other local minima exist, for example near (t₀, t_f) = (200, 970) d with ΔV ≈ 7.96 km/s, which is nearly as efficient and is not constrained by the edge of the search window.

4. Discussion

Compared with the reference case, the minimum-cost transfer cuts the total ΔV from 20.03 to 7.86 km/s, and its timing aligns the spacecraft's arrival velocity much more closely with Didymos's heliocentric velocity. Several caveats apply:

  • The global minimum lies on the t_f = 1000 d boundary of the search window, so a longer window could reveal slightly better solutions
  • The zero-sphere-of-influence model treats ΔV_Earth as the departure hyperbolic excess change; a real mission would add the cost of escaping Earth's gravity well from a parking orbit, and the arrival ΔV would involve a rendezvous with Didymos's very weak gravity field
  • The model assumes unperturbed two-body motion and only prograde, zero-revolution transfers
  • The 5-day grid limits the precision of the optimum, and a local optimizer starting from the grid minimum would refine it

5. Conclusion

Using Keplerian propagation and a universal-variable Lambert solver, the heliocentric states of Earth and Didymos were computed and transfers between them evaluated. The reference 400-day transfer costs 20.0300 km/s, while a porkchop search finds a transfer of 7.8600 km/s (departure t_ref + 600 d, arrival t_ref + 1000 d). Future work should include longer search windows, multi-revolution solutions, and patched-conic modeling of the planetary departure and arrival phases.

Appendix A — MATLAB Script

Main driver script for Problem 4. Supporting functions (rv2kep, kep2rv, ta2ma, ma2ta, propagate_elliptical, lambert_universal, stumpff) were submitted separately in the code archive.

%% Problem 4: Mission to Didymos
clear; clc; close all;

%Constants
AU = 149.6e6;        % km per AU
MU = 132712000000;   % km^3/s^2
day2sec = 86400;     % s/day
eps = 1e-8;          % Stumpff tolerance
tol = 1e-8;          % Lambert tolerance

%Define
Earth = [1.00000011*AU; 0.016710212; deg2rad(0.00005);
         deg2rad(-11.26064); deg2rad(102.94719); deg2rad(100.46435)];

Didymos = [1.6442*AU; 0.38385; deg2rad(3.4079);
           deg2rad(73.196); deg2rad(319.321); deg2rad(195.0)];

% PART (a): Earth state at t = t_ref + 12 days
dt_a = 12 * day2sec;
rv_earth_a = propagate_elliptical(Earth, dt_a, MU);
r_earth_a = rv_earth_a(1:3);
v_earth_a = rv_earth_a(4:6);

% PART (b): Didymos state at t = t_ref + 412 days
dt_b = 412 * day2sec;
rv_did_b = propagate_elliptical(Didymos, dt_b, MU);
r_did_b = rv_did_b(1:3);
v_did_b = rv_did_b(4:6);

%PART (c): Lambert transfer (t0 = +12d, tf = +412d, TOF = 400d)
tof_c = (412 - 12) * day2sec;
[v1_c, v2_c, res_c] = lambert_universal(r_earth_a, r_did_b, tof_c, MU, 0, eps, tol);
dV_earth_c = norm(v1_c - v_earth_a);
dV_didymos_c = norm(v2_c - v_did_b);
dV_total_c = dV_earth_c + dV_didymos_c;

% Transfer arc propagation
rv_sc0 = [r_earth_a; v1_c];
N_arc = 500;
t_arc = linspace(0, tof_c, N_arc);
r_arc_c = zeros(3, N_arc);
for k = 1:N_arc
    rv_k = propagate_elliptical(rv_sc0, t_arc(k), MU);
    r_arc_c(:,k) = rv_k(1:3);
end

%PART (d): Porkchop plot (t_ref to t_ref+1000 days, skip if tf-t0<=100)
t_grid = (0:5:1000);
N_g = length(t_grid);
DV_mat = NaN(N_g, N_g);

r_e_all = zeros(3, N_g); v_e_all = zeros(3, N_g);
r_d_all = zeros(3, N_g); v_d_all = zeros(3, N_g);
for k = 1:N_g
    rv_e = propagate_elliptical(Earth, t_grid(k)*day2sec, MU);
    rv_d = propagate_elliptical(Didymos, t_grid(k)*day2sec, MU);
    r_e_all(:,k) = rv_e(1:3); v_e_all(:,k) = rv_e(4:6);
    r_d_all(:,k) = rv_d(1:3); v_d_all(:,k) = rv_d(4:6);
end

for i = 1:N_g
    for j = i+1 : N_g
        tof_days = t_grid(j) - t_grid(i);
        if tof_days <= 100
            continue
        end
        try
            [v1_ij, v2_ij, ~] = lambert_universal( ...
                r_e_all(:,i), r_d_all(:,j), tof_days*day2sec, MU, 0, eps, tol);
            dv = norm(v1_ij - v_e_all(:,i)) + norm(v2_ij - v_d_all(:,j));
            if isfinite(dv)
                DV_mat(i,j) = dv;
            end
        catch
            % skip failed solves
        end
    end
end

%Global minimum
[~, idx] = min(DV_mat(:));
[ri, ci] = ind2sub(size(DV_mat), idx);
t0_best = t_grid(ri);
tf_best = t_grid(ci);
DV_best = DV_mat(ri,ci);
fprintf("Global minimum: t0=%g day, tf=%g day, DV=%.4f km/s\n", t0_best, tf_best, DV_best);

% PART (e): Best Transfer
tof_e = (tf_best - t0_best) * day2sec;
rv_earth_e = propagate_elliptical(Earth, t0_best*day2sec, MU);
rv_did_e = propagate_elliptical(Didymos, tf_best*day2sec, MU);
r_earth_e = rv_earth_e(1:3); v_earth_e = rv_earth_e(4:6);
r_did_e = rv_did_e(1:3); v_did_e = rv_did_e(4:6);

[v1_e, v2_e, res_e] = lambert_universal(r_earth_e, r_did_e, tof_e, MU, 0, eps, tol);
dV_e = norm(v1_e - v_earth_e) + norm(v2_e - v_did_e);
fprintf("Best transfer total DV = %.4f km/s\n", dV_e);
